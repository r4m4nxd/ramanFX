## 1. Probability / odds enhancer score sheet (pp. 2–5)

Maximum total is 10. There are six enhancers:

| Enhancer | Max | Rule (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| Strength of the move (departure) | 2 | How fast price left the level. A faster exit means a bigger imbalance. A strong fashion scores 2, a gradual drift scores less. |
| Reward / risk (profit margin) | 2 | (a) How far price ran from the level before it came back (the initial profit margin). (b) Distance to the nearest opposing level. Example: risk 20 cents → the opposing level must be at least 60 cents away (≥ 3:1), otherwise **no trade**. |
| Big picture | 2 | Daily chart, even for intraday trades. 2 = the trade is with the big-picture trend. 0–1 = against the trend AND near big-picture opposing S/D ("don't short right above a demand level"). |
| Retracements / tests (freshness) | 2 | **1st return = 2, 2nd return = 1, 3rd return = 0.** "Motion into mass": each test removes orders (the tree-chopping analogy). |
| Time at level | 1 | **Fewer candles at the base is better.** Long basing means equilibrium, which is not tradable. Compare with other bases on the same timeframe; there is no fixed count, because the timeframe changes the count. |
| Arrival | 1 | How price comes back to the level. No new opposing S/D should form on the way in (no new resistance just above a buy, no new support just below a sell). "Strong arrival usually means strong departure." |

**Score → action** (as given in the notes):
- **9–10 → limit order** (resting entry at the level).
- **8 → confirmation entry** ("the worst possible case is an 8").
- **7 or lower → no trade** (the text says "no trade if less than 7"; the table treats 7 as the cutoff).

**Extra enhancer for short-term traders: time of day.** Setups that reach entry in the **first 45–60 minutes of the session** are "golden". Imbalance is largest at or near the open. Once the opening orders are worked through, S/D is back in balance, and afternoon trading is harder. Novices should not trade the open; skilled traders should.

## 2. XLT trading notes (pp. 5–9)

- **Patterns.** Supply = Rally-Base-Drop. Demand = Drop-Base-Rally.
- **Trend playbook.** Uptrend: buy pullbacks into quality demand. Downtrend: short rallies into supply. Sideways: do both, selling at supply and buying at demand.
- "The more times price comes to the level ... the more likely there is a turn" (p. 5). This is a stray line that seems to contradict the freshness rule. It most likely means a turn *through* the level (the level breaks).
- **Four market stages** (Weinstein, *Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets*, using the **30-period weighted MA**): Stage 1 accumulation range after a selloff → Stage 2 uptrend → Stage 3 distribution / top range ("institutions selling to John Q Public", with upgrades and talk of super-spikes) → Stage 4 decline.
- **Top-down order.** Monthly → Weekly → Daily → Hourly → 5-minute. Two reasons: where price sits relative to big-picture S/D, and what the big-picture trend is.
- **"We don't cut through candles."** Draw levels from real-time S/D bases, not lines that slice through candles.
- **Entries.** "Understanding where the turning points are is everything." Most stop or target problems are really entry problems.
- **Three entry types:** limit, confirmation, breakout. The odds-enhancer score decides which one, and strength of the move matters most. XLT members often take only limit entries on the strongest levels.
- **Confirmation mechanics** (make it a mechanical rule):
  - Let price enter the level, then enter as it **leaves** the level.
  - Trigger = a fixed offset such as **$0.01 / $0.05 / $0.10 beyond the level**, or a trigger inside the level. Any rule is fine as long as it is fixed. If the offset ruins R:R, skip the trade.
  - Sequence: trigger hit → the entry order is armed. If entry fills first, put in the stop. **If price reaches the stop price before entry fills → cancel; there is no trade.** If price goes through the level, cancel.
- **Time at level, rule of thumb.** For a 240-minute base of 2 candles, drop to the 60-minute chart to find the base. "**3–6 candles is probably the best on any time frame**". But the key is a **fast exit**: "There must be a fast leave of the level, or I won't look at the level."
- **Rotation logic.** If price cannot go up and test supply, it has to go back and test the next demand (and vice versa). If a level breaks, look left for the next one.
- **Only two reasons for a turn:** sellers outnumber buyers at supply (or buyers dry up), and the mirror case at demand.

## 3. Newsletter rules (pp. 9–80)

### Profit margin and R:R
- Profit margin = the distance from the entry level to the opposing level. "Look to your left": before you buy, check that supply is far above; before you sell, check that demand is far below.
- **The initial profit margin must be at least 3 × the stop (≥ 3:1 to the first target).** Price often runs past target 1, but 3:1 gets the trade started.
- Each level is judged by how far price ran away from it before it came back.

### Strength of departure
- A strong departure (free fall, gap, big candles) means a large imbalance, and price will likely fall or rise fast on the **first** revisit. A slow drift may still be tradable, but the imbalance is smaller.
- Gaps out of a base are a strong departure (DOW mini, 10Y note examples).

### Fresh levels and retests
- Trade only the **first** revisit, because risk is lowest and reward highest then. Every later pullback lowers the odds.
- SPY example (2008): "support 1" had been revisited many times, which makes it **weaker, not stronger** (each visit fills buyers). Prefer the deeper, untouched "support 2", which was the origin of the move and sat near the 200 MA.

### Location on the supply/demand curve
- A level is "well placed on the curve" when it is **far from equilibrium**, meaning low in the big-picture range for demand or high for supply. That gives a large margin.
- A good intraday level that is badly placed on the big-picture curve is a **day-trade bounce only, not a swing** (S&P example).
- A supply level very high on the curve (CAD, crude) → treat it as a **swing** trade, because the margin is huge.
- **Old levels are fine and often better.** A 2003 demand level in XLF still worked in 2008. Old levels sit far from current equilibrium, so they are well placed on the curve. The orders are not the same ones; the price itself draws new buyers.

### Entry, stop and target specifics (worked examples)
- S&P supply 1465–1471.50: let price rally into the zone and **sell as it declines from the level (about 1464)**. Stop **just above the level (1472)**, target the demand at about 1430.
- CAD supply .9500–.9515: entry .9495–.9498, stop .9517. **The stop is about 2 ticks beyond the zone and the entry is just outside the proximal** (a confirmation-style entry).
- Crude supply 75.85–76.30: entry 75.80–75.85, stop 76.35.
- NVLS: short 31.00–31.35, stop 31.46, first objective 1.50–3.00 (about 5–10R). IBM: buy 108.10–109.10, stop 107.75, objective 3.00–6.00.
- Silver: buy the first pullback into demand with the stop just below the level.
- "The closer the entry is to the stop, the larger the position size can be for the same capital at risk."

### Day-trading rules ("Important Rules to Follow")
1. Enter only at a demand or supply level, at any time of day.
2. Two entry types: **first-pullback** (lower risk, preferred) and **breakout**. Both need a fresh level plus a large profit zone.
3. Every day, mark one demand and one supply level per market on a **larger intraday timeframe**, and always know the big-picture S/D.
4. Only trade setups with a **≥ 3:1 profit zone to the first target**.
5. **Pre-plan and pre-set** entry, stop and targets.
6. Ignore news, lagging indicators and subjective information. "Any and all influences on price are reflected in price."

### The A-B-C logic of a level (OTA Futures article)
- A = a pause that only looks like equilibrium. B = a sharp move away, which proves A was imbalanced. C = the first return to A, which is the low-risk entry. A more dramatic B means a bigger reaction is expected at C.
- Novice candle sequence (supply): A = balance; B = a close below the range; C = a novice buying the first rally back, **on above-average volume**, into untouched supply; **D's close confirms; enter on E below the low of D's body** (p. 51).

### Novice-trader profile (who we take the other side of)
- They buy **after** a rally (late, high risk) **and** into supply (low odds), often against the larger trend (very low odds). Sellers do the mirror.
- Gap ups after a big advance, big green candles late in a move, and broker upgrades into supply are all signs of novice buying.

### Trade management
- XLF: after +$2.00, **move the stop to breakeven** and take some or all profit at the next opposing supply.
- If the stop is wide (a wide drawn zone), **cut the position size** so the risk stays inside your maximum.

### Chart patterns (The Qualifier)
- Triangles, flags, pennants, cup-and-handle and H&S all use **breakout** entries. The result depends on the room to the next opposing level. RIMM triangle about 5:1 (take); DRYS, support close below (weak).
- **Less time spent in the pattern = better outcome** (the same idea as time at level).
- Do not chase breakout fills. If you miss the low-risk entry, let it go.

### Opening gap trade (Morning Gap, parts 1 and 2)
- Lowest-risk, highest-reward trade of the day: **the open gaps into an objective level**. A gap up into supply after an advance → short. A gap down into demand after a decline → long. Entry within seconds to minutes of the open.
- Morning prep, about 15 minutes: scan for stocks opening at large imbalances plus broker upgrades and downgrades. An upgrade that gaps price into supply is a fade (IMCL, 3/19).
- "By the end of the first hour ... novice capital is transferred."

### Moving-average cross as a level marker (DIS swing)
- Fast and slow MAs usually **cross at the origin of the imbalance**. The first return to the cross price often holds as S/D: "extend a line to the right and wait." The **200 MA slope** shows trend (down-sloping 200 MA → shorting at supply is OK).

### Indicators
- Price only: indicators lag, and lag adds risk. Indicators are fine if they are used inside S/D rules (an archived CCI article is referenced; the student RIMM trade used CCI overbought plus a double top at the prior day's high).

### Drawing the zone lines (Q&A)
- Draw the lines so that **most of the trading in the base is between them**. Wicks and bodies change with the timeframe, so check two timeframes. High-to-low lines are fine but give a bigger stop; then shrink the size.

### Plan questions (2008 article)
- Do you have a plan and follow it? Is there discretion in it (turn it into rules)? Is your analysis objective? Can you quantify S/D? Do you know your average win / average loss and win %?

### Not trading rules (context only)
- "The Dollar, In Real Money Terms": the Dow in dollar-index terms (2000: 11,700 × 1.24 = 14,508; 2007: 13,500 × 0.81 = 10,935, a 25% real decline). The NASDAQ fell about 66% in real terms. Dow/gold ratio 47:1 (2000) → 19:1 (2007). Interest rates and the dollar shown on S/D charts.
- "Think Like a Goose": news and fundamentals are illusion. When perceived risk is lowest, actual risk is often highest. Strong news drives one side to exhaust itself into an S/D level (London bombing day reversed at demand).

## Proposals for ZoneVision / ZoneBot

These are filters and inputs only; zone drawing stays as it is (the parity rule in SDR). Each one is switchable for A/B tests. They do not duplicate SDR #1-17 except where noted.

1. **Seiden score → entry type.** Compute the six-enhancer score: departure 0-2, margin 0-2, big picture 0-2, tests 2/1/0, time at base 0-1, arrival 0-1.
   - Score ≥ `InpSeidenLimitMin = 9` → resting limit at the proximal.
   - Score = `InpSeidenConfMin = 8` → confirmation entry only (see #2).
   - Score < 8 → skip.
   - Input `InpEntryBySeidenScore = false` keeps the current behaviour by default. This overlaps SDR #5 (a score as a gate), but adds the score-to-entry-type mapping.
2. **Leave-the-zone confirmation trigger.** Once price trades inside the zone, place a stop entry at proximal − `InpConfOffsetFrac = 0.05` × zone height (buy: above the proximal by that amount). Cancel if the distal-plus-buffer stop price trades before the fill, or if R:R after the offset is below `InpZ3RR`.
   - `InpConfMode = 0` (off) / 1 (offset trigger).
3. **Graded freshness instead of a binary block.** Count the returns that reached the proximal: 0 → score 2, 1 → score 1, ≥ 2 → 0 or skip.
   - `InpMaxTests = 0` (default = current fresh-only); allow 1 for testing.
4. **Arrival filter.** Skip if a new opposing zone (any ETF zone of the opposite type) formed between the swing that started the return and the proximal.
   - `InpArrivalClean = true`. Give +1 when the arrival leg is impulsive (`InpArrivalLegRatio = 1.8`, the same leg metric as departure).
5. **Initial profit margin (run-out).** The distance price ran from the zone before its first return, divided by the planned risk, must be ≥ `InpMinRunOutR = 3.0`. This is the run-out from the zone, not the room to the opposing zone, which is SDR #3.
6. **Time at base, relative to other bases.** Score +1 when baseN ≤ the median baseN of the last `InpBaseLookback = 20` zones on the same TF. Keep the hard cap at 6 (ST:144).
7. **Opening-hour bonus.** +1 score (or allow a lower min score) when the fill would happen within `InpOpenWindowMin = 60` minutes of the session open (London 07:00 UTC, NY 13:30 UTC for indices / 12:00 UTC for FX).
   - `InpOpenBonus = true`. This complements SDR #11, which only gives an allowed window.
8. **Gap-into-level at the open** (indices / stocks; CFDs with a weekend gap for FX).
   - If the first bar of the session opens inside a fresh opposing zone after a ≥ `InpGapMinATR = 0.5` ATR gap in the trend direction, arm a confirmation entry (#2) to fade it.
   - `InpGapFade = false`.
9. **Curve → holding horizon.** If depth on the HTF curve is ≤ 33% (demand) or ≥ 67% (supply), use the opposing HTF zone as the target (swing). Otherwise cap the TP at `InpZ3RR` (bounce only).
   - `InpCurveTarget = true`. This builds on SDR #6.
10. **Novice-volume tag (ZoneVision note only).** Flag "late buyers into supply" when tick volume on the arrival candle is > 1.5 × the 20-bar average (`InpVolNoteMult = 1.5`). Display only; not a gate until it is backtested.

Note: Kevin's own lessons (VL:1708-1711) argue against mechanical score sheets. Keep #1 as an optional preset (for example `SEIDEN`) instead of replacing the KEVIN_BEST logic.
