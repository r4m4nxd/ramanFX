# RamanFX supply & demand strategy: summary for review

Hand this file to another AI (Grok, Gemini, …) together with the prompt at the end. Bring the answer back for review.

## 1. The idea
Price leaves a level fast when big orders were left unfilled there. A **demand zone** (buy area) is a short pause (the base)
followed by an explosive move up; a **supply zone** (sell area) is a pause followed by an explosive move down. When price
comes back the first time, the leftover orders push it away again. We buy demand low and sell supply high, with the
bigger-timeframe trend, with the stop just beyond the zone and the target at the next opposing zone.

Mentors: **Kevin** (video course, primary), **Sam Seiden** (OTA), **Kevin Baker** ("No Stinkin' Indicators"), **Raja**
(trend-change and arrival rules).

## 2. Drawing a zone
- **Find the strong move first**, then the base it came from (1-3 candles is best, up to 6; fewer = stronger).
- **Four parts** (Kevin): base, explosive leg-out, a candle that stays away, and space before the proximal line.
- **Proximal line** on the near edge of the base bodies; **distal line** on the extreme wick of base + leg.
- Patterns: drop-base-rally / rally-base-drop (reversal) and rally-base-rally / drop-base-drop (continuation; Kevin trades them, Seiden does not).
- **Qualified** (Kevin): the leg-out must remove an opposing zone or a trend line, or make a new extreme.
- **Fresh only**: the first return is the trade. A zone that price has entered is used; a body close through it removes it.
- Zone height similar to other zones; an oversized zone is refined on a lower timeframe.

## 3. Odds enhancers (quality)
Strong departure (big candles, gaps best) · few base candles · fresh · far run-out before the return (profit margin) ·
**location on the curve** (demand low in the big-picture range, supply high) · higher timeframe agrees · a liquidity trap
(an obvious level) just in front of the zone · fast, clean arrival (no new opposing zone on the way back).

## 4. Timeframes (top-down)
- **Forex, metals, indices:** monthly → weekly → daily → 4-hour → hourly (15-minute for confirmation).
- **Stocks:** 3-month → monthly → weekly → daily; stocks are **long only** (supply = where to take profit).
- Entry-TF → higher-TF pairs (Kevin): 15m→H4, H1→D1, H4→W1, D1→MN; confirmation one step lower (H4→15m, D1→H1).
- **Nesting:** the best trade is a lower-TF zone sitting inside a higher-TF zone in the trend's direction.
- Never buy into higher-TF supply or sell into higher-TF demand.

## 5. Trend and direction change (Raja)
- Trend = an obvious trend line (peak + valley + new extreme). Trade pullbacks with it; counter-trend only at major fresh HTF zones, smaller size.
- **Trend lines:** anchored only on the best pivot candles inside zones that made the higher high / lower low (owner's rule).
- **Direction change** in 3 steps: (1) a candle **closes** through the trend line, (2) the first opposite zone is removed,
  (3) the second opposite zone is removed → direction changed. Then trade a fresh zone in the new direction.

## 6. Arrival and realignment (Raja, "the A+ trade")
A higher-TF pullback should end in a **realignment area**: the weak zone nearest the bigger trend line. When price is in it,
wait for a **lower-TF arrival**: the lower timeframe turns (its trend line breaks and an opposite zone is removed), leaving a
fresh lower-TF zone. Enter there (confirmation entry). Take profit at the last higher-TF swing.

## 7. Entry, stop, target, management
- **Entry:** limit order just in front of the proximal line (set-and-forget), or a confirmation entry as above. Never chase.
- **Stop:** just beyond the distal line (+ spread); never widened. If the stop is too big, trade smaller or skip.
- **Target:** the next opposing zone; at least **3× the risk** (2× accepted when the structure only offers that).
- **Management:** break-even after ~2R, partial at 3R, trail behind new lower-TF zones.
- **Risk:** about 1% per trade (0.5% on lower-odds setups); a few positions at a time; stop after a losing streak.
- Cancel a pending order when the move already happened or the opposing HTF zone was hit first. Avoid trading through big news.

## 8. Long-term (TFSA) rule
Buy stocks at the **best price**: inside, near (coming down to) or just leaving a weekly / monthly / 3-month demand zone,
biggest timeframe first. Extra weight when company insiders are buying at the zone, or when the weekly downtrend line just
broke and a fresh daily demand zone formed.

## 9. Open questions / known weak spots
- Break-even timing (1R vs 2R vs 3R) and fixed 3R target vs "just before the next zone".
- Graded freshness (Seiden: depth of the first return ≥ 25% = used) vs any touch = used.
- Continuation zones (RBR/DBD): keep or drop?
- Counter-trend trades: allowed only at fresh HTF zones? with what size?
- Whether both trend-line anchors must be strict pivots.
- Score sheet as a gate (Seiden 10-point) vs Kevin's "tradable or not".
- Volatility floor on stops for gold / oil vs tight structural stops.
- Cross-market checks (US dollar index for FX, S&P 500 for stocks) are not used yet.
