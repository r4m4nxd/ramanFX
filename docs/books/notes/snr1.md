## 2. Glossary (book definitions, paraphrased)

- **SNR level**: horizontal level of one of three types. It acts as support when price is above it and resistance when below (p17–19). The charts draw it at the body junction (open/close), not at the wick, and it matches the line-chart turning point (p20–22, p24).
- **A-shape level**: a bullish candle followed by a bearish candle, i.e. a peak. Resistance. Once broken it becomes support ("A-shape broken level") (p17–19).
- **V-shape level**: a bearish candle followed by a bullish candle, i.e. a trough. Support. Once broken it becomes resistance (p17–19).
- **Gap level**: two candles in the same direction. The level is the close of the first / open of the second (p17, p22). Bullish gap = support, bearish gap = resistance (p18–19). (The definition is minimal. It reads like a continuation candle junction, not a true price gap. Unclear.)
- **Fresh level**: never touched since it formed, OR broken by a candle body. A body break makes it fresh again (p23–25, p200 "becomes fresh again, restarting the cycle").
- **Unfresh level**: a wick touched it but no body broke it (p23, p25). Rule: "Always look at the end of the levels."
- **Rejection**: price touches a fresh level and turns away. Whether a candle close is needed depends on the TF: M/W no close needed ("still strong"); D, 4H, 30m, 15m need that TF's close; 1H needs the **4H** close (p93). Candle colour does not matter (p96).
- **CC (Confirmation Candle)**: "the third candle that forms immediately after the level is created". It must point the same way as the level (green on support, red on resistance). An unfresh level with a CC still counts as valid (p96). p139 also calls the CC the "first valid time" a level is used.
- **Trendline (TL)**: a straight line through 2+ V points (uptrend/support) or A points (downtrend/resistance). "Don't force them" (p28). The charts draw TLs on the line chart (closes) (p115–118, p145).
- **TL types** (p29 diagram): *Reversal TL* (a line along the swings of the current trend; touching it reverses the trend), *Outer TL* (a line along the counter swings, touched from outside), *QM TL* (a near-flat line through the first H/L that price returns to after the HH/LL), *Break & Retest TL* (a broken TL retested from the other side). Each has bullish and bearish versions. My descriptions come from the sketches only; the text does not define them.
- **Bullish/Bearish breakout**: price breaks above resistance / below support (p32–33).
- **QM (Quasimodo)**: Bearish = H, L, HH, then LL breaks below L. Bullish = L, H, LL, then HH breaks above H (p34–35). "For any breakout to be valid… it must form a QM pattern" (p34). Part 1 = the old-trend leg up to the extreme; part 2 = the new leg that breaks part 1 (p67).
- **External breakout**: breaks the last A (bullish) / V (bearish) that formed **before** the touch of the fresh level or TL. **Internal breakout**: breaks an A/V that formed **after** the touch (p37, p45). On SNR levels only external breakouts are valid (p38–44, p67, p74).
- **Last touch**: on SNR, the breakout must break structure from before the last touch, and a QM formed after the touch is invalid. On a TL, a QM after the touch is valid and early breakouts are fine (p74–75).
- **Early breakout (rare MTF)**: a breakout on a TF one step higher than the normal confirmation TF (e.g. a weekly BO at a monthly level, before the daily BO). Caused by hidden LTF V/A levels inside long HTF wicks (p121–123).
- **Drawdown (TL)**: the wick pierces an HTF trendline before the real breakout. This is normal (p150–151).
- **Opposite-side (rare) TL breakout**: price breaks the "wrong" side of a TL first (manipulation) and then makes the real move (p153).
- **Continuation breakout**: a breakout without a QM or left shoulder, inside an existing trend. It confirms continuation. Sometimes it forms a QM (rare, extra confirmation) (p179–184).
- **Trend**: bullish / bearish / consolidation (p65–66). A trend change requires a QM breakout (p67).
- **Storyline**: the logical flow of price between SNR levels and TLs across timeframes (p203). Phases: Beginning (from a fresh level) → Decision (touch, then rejection or breakout) → Confirmation (retest and continue, or a new setup and reversal) (p205).
- **Classic Storyline**: a fresh level, then a rejection candle on that TF, then a breakout on the confirmation TF ("1+2+3", p213). It runs from a fresh level to the next level (p210–230).
- **Mini Classic Storyline**: the same thing on 4H/1H/30m/15m. SNR levels only (p229–230).
- **Advanced Storyline** (the author claims he invented it): used when no fresh levels remain. HTF trendline plus a breakout on the confirmation TF ("1+2", no rejection-candle step). Runs from trendline to trendline (p238–266).
- **Continuation Storyline**: after a confirmed shift, price keeps going. It is confirmed by repeated same-direction breakouts on the confirmation TF at fresh levels or TLs (p291–297).
- **MTF Storylines**: nested storylines. Inside a monthly level-to-level move, price travels weekly level to weekly level, and so on down (p300–312).
- **MTF Alignment**: daily, weekly and monthly levels/TLs in the same zone. The lower TF breaks first (1H before 4H, i.e. the daily storyline before the weekly), and this gives the earliest entry into the HTF move (p313–320).

Terms **not** found in pp1–320: MPL, SBR/RBS (only listed in the TOC for Ch.5.5), DMD, SISD, BSD, Danger Zone, Road Block, Blindspot, Hidden Confirmation, MEF, Engulf, Flag limit, Compression. Also from the TOC for later chapters: ERS (Early Right Shoulder, 4.24/5.6), QMS, Apex, LS, GAP QMS, "5Min EMA confirmation" (5.11). These are probably in snr2+.

## 3. Rules (with page refs)

### Drawing
- Levels are drawn at the candle-body junction (open/close) of the two-candle pattern: A = bull candle then bear candle; V = bear candle then bull candle; Gap = two same-direction candles (p17, charts p20–24). Each chart is paired with a line chart, and the levels coincide with line-chart turning points. This implies closes and bodies define levels, not wicks (my reading of the charts; the text never says "body" for drawing).
- Freshness is judged by what happens to the right of the level: a wick touch makes it unfresh; a body break or no touch keeps it fresh (p23–25).
- Trendlines: 2+ V points (up) or A points (down), kept simple (p28). The charts draw them on closes / the line chart (p115–118, p145, p155).
- Only the three level types are used (p17).

### Validity
- Breakout = a strong candle **body close** beyond the level. A wick-only break is invalid (p35, p37, p45; the p52 quiz implies the same).
- On a trendline, the body must close beyond both the TL and the relevant A/V shape level (p45).
- A breakout must form a QM (p34), except a continuation breakout (p179).
- SNR level: break the **last A (bullish) / last V (bearish) formed before the touch** (external). Breaks of A/V formed after the touch are invalid (p37–44, p71–72, p74).
- Trendline: a QM after the touch and early breakouts are both valid (p74–75).
- Rare LTF case: on 4H/1H/30m/15m SNR, a break of the last A/V formed after the touch can be valid (p126). This contradicts p74 for the LTFs (it is presented as a rare exception).
- Uses per level: SNR level valid twice (the CC, then a touch plus breakout), and it weakens after that (p139). A body breakout makes it fresh again (p200). Trendline: up to 5 touches, the 3rd strongest, the 4th and 5th valid only with a confirmed breakout (p139, p192).
- When an SNR level and a TL align (M/W/D only), the TL takes priority. A TL breakout is enough, and an SNR breakout without a TL break is also valid (p129–130).
- Wording slip: the p192 recap says "The third touch is the most valid one **for entries**". p139 says "for a breakout".

### Rejection, by TF (p93)
M: no close needed · W: no close needed · D: daily close · 4H: 4H close · **1H: 4H close** · 30m: 30m close · 15m: 15m close.

### Timeframe sequence (level TF → confirmation breakout TF) (p99, p114, p163, p195)
| Level / TL TF | Breakout TF | Early (rare) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | Daily | Weekly | SNR or TL |
| Weekly | 4H | Daily | SNR or TL |
| Daily | 1H | 4H | SNR or TL |
| 4H | 1H / 30m / 15m | – | SNR only |
| 1H | 30m / 15m | – | SNR only |
| 30m | 5m | – | SNR only |
| 15m | 1m | – | SNR only |
- Drawdown map for TLs: M TL → W or D BO; W TL → D or 4H; D TL → 4H or 1H (p150).
- Inconsistency: the Weekly Advanced text (p250–251) says a **daily** breakout confirms, with 4H as the alternative. Daily Advanced (p258–259) says a daily breakout confirmed by 4H. The diagrams (p252, p260), p239 and the summary on p270 all say **Weekly TL → 4H BO** and **Daily TL → 1H BO**. Treat the table above as canonical.

### Direction and trend change
- HTF direction = a QM breakout on the confirmation TF at a fresh HTF level or TL. Example: a bullish daily QM breakout after a bearish daily trend at a monthly SNR/TL means a bullish monthly direction (p161–163).
- Storyline start (Classic) = fresh level + rejection candle + confirmation-TF breakout (p210–230). Advanced = HTF TL + confirmation-TF breakout (p239, p244).
- Priority: Classic while fresh levels exist; Advanced when none remain; both when they align, taking the TL break as the early signal and the SNR break as extra confirmation (p269–271).
- MTF: strongest when M/W/D all agree. With mixed signals, use caution and wait. "Avoid Trading Against Alignment" (p304–305, p317).

### Entry / SL / TP / management
- **Not covered in pp1–320.** The TOC places entries, confirmations, SL and TP in Ch.5 (5.9–5.14) and Ch.6.13. The only entry-flavoured statements here: "Enter early, before the big move starts", by spotting the daily (or 1H) breakout at aligned levels (p317), and "4H and 1H breakouts help refine entry points" (p317).

### When NOT to trade (as stated or implied)
- On a wick-only breakout, or a breakout without a QM (unless it is a continuation) (p35, p37).
- On internal breakouts at SNR levels, or a QM formed after the touch on an SNR level (p67, p74).
- When the breakout TF does not match the level TF ("Misalignment… can lead to unreliable signals", p99, p114).
- On an SNR level used more than twice (p139), or a TL past its 5th touch (p139).
- Against MTF alignment, e.g. daily bullish while W and M are bearish (p317). With mixed signals, wait (p305).

## 6. Representative chart examples
1. **p24, XAUUSD Daily, Dec 2023–Mar 2024**: freshness. An A-shape near 2077 was touched by a later wick, so it is unfresh. An A-shape near 2055 was never touched, so it is fresh. A bearish gap near 2040 was wick-touched (unfresh) and then broken by a body (fresh again). A V-shape near 1992 was never touched (fresh). The early-March rally body-breaks 2040, 2055 and 2077, making each fresh again.
2. **p39–41, XAUUSD (TF not stated), "FALSE SIGNALS"**: at each circled touch of a resistance level, the last V low before the touch is ticked as the valid bearish breakout level. Smaller V lows formed after the touch are crossed out (internal = false). Every example is shown on candles and on a line chart.
3. **p68–70, XAUUSD 4H**: bullish QM after a bearish trend. Part 1 is the drop to about 1985; part 2 breaks the prior high near 1993; then a bullish trend to about 2040. Bearish QM at about 2480 (17–18th): part 1 is the high; part 2 breaks the low near 2465; then a bearish trend to about 2380.
4. **p100–101, XAUUSD Monthly→Daily**: a fresh monthly V level at about 1810 gets a monthly candle rejection (long wick). On the daily, price dips to the level and the "daily BO" breaks the last A before the touch → bullish. The mirror case: a fresh monthly A level at about 1985, weekly rejection, then a daily bearish BO.
5. **p164–165, XAUUSD Monthly + Daily**: a fresh monthly level at about 1820 coincides with a "monthly QM trendline". A bearish daily trend reaches it ("reject monthly"), then a daily bullish BO near 1925 → bullish daily trend to about 2075. This illustrates SNR+TL alignment and the classic storyline start.
6. **p170, XAUUSD 4H**: a bearish 4H trend into a fresh weekly level (about 1800) plus a weekly break & retest TL (about 1790). "Reject weekly", then a 4H bullish BO at about 1815 → bullish 4H trend to about 1870, which starts the weekly storyline.
7. **p215, XAUUSD Monthly + Daily (around 2018–19 prices)**: the monthly storyline runs from a fresh monthly level at about 1180 to another at about 1325. A bullish daily breakout at the bottom starts the monthly bullish classic storyline. A bearish daily breakout at the top starts the bearish one.
8. **p320, XAUUSD Daily + 1H**: everything labelled at once. A bearish monthly classic storyline starts at about 1935 (the monthly level plus a TL); a 4H BO at the weekly level (about 1880) starts the weekly bearish storyline; 1H BOs start the bearish daily classic and advanced storylines; price falls to about 1805, where the "bullish monthly classic storyline started".
No example in this range shows an entry price, SL, TP or trade result.
