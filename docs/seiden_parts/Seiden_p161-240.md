## 1. Top-down alignment (pp.161-163)

- Order of work: broad market (S&P) → 3-4 sectors (XLF here) → 3 big stocks in the best sector (BAC). Take the stock whose demand level **lines up** with the sector and index demand.
- A gap away from a level means a bigger imbalance at its origin, and a stronger move away means a better level.
- The plan was made the day before, and price gapped down into the level the next day. **Risk was only $0.15** on BAC.
- The seller at the level is a "novice": they are selling *after a decline* and *into demand* (Seiden's "two mistakes"; the phrase recurs throughout).
- He prefers manual top-down scanning to automated scanners; it is faster for him.

## 2. The Odds Enhancers (pp.163-167)

- Not every prior turn is S/R. Look for the levels where demand and supply are **most** out of balance.
- Scoring: the **maximum is 10**, and each enhancer has a maximum score. The three explained here:
  1. **Strength of the move** (departure): **2/2** when price left fast and strong; less for a "gradual descent".
  2. **Profit margin** (R:R): compare the stop distance with the distance from entry to the **nearest opposing level (target 1)**. **≥ 3:1 → 2/2.**
  3. **Big picture**: go to the higher TF, find its **fresh** levels, and do not short anywhere near HTF demand. Ideally short close to HTF supply ("correct side of the curve"). Well placed → 2/2.
  - "Three more" enhancers are not covered in this part. In pp.1-80 they are tests/freshness, time at base and arrival.
- **Score 9 → limit entry, with every order for the whole trade placed before the fill** (entry, stop, targets).
- Use the score sheet for a while as a new member, then learn to "eyeball" it.
- Gold example: short at 864. Exits on 2 contracts at **852 and 835** (835 was demand). After target 1 the stop was "pulled up" so the trade could not lose. **Risk $1,200 → profit $4,100.**
- Psychology: a "grander vision" (family, life) gives the discipline to wait for low-risk/high-reward setups instead of feeling the "urge to trade".

## 3. Trading psychology: invitations to buy or sell (pp.167-171)

- News, indicators, earnings and upgrades/downgrades are "invitations" to trade. Filter every one through the actual S/D picture on the chart before acting.
- The belief system *is* the strategy. "Lack of discipline" is really a conflict between the invitations you accept and what you believe.
- Two rules:
  - Negative news that brings price **into peak demand** → usually a **buy**.
  - Positive news that brings price **into peak supply** → usually a **sell**.
- BAC on 21 April: there was bad news, and he bought at demand anyway.
- "A cluster of trading activity" (conventional S/R) is **not** necessarily true S/D. A specific pattern (base + strong departure) is.

## 4. Governing dynamics (pp.171-174)

- This is philosophy: keep things simple. Supply/demand = Newton's "mass", and price "motion" runs from a demand imbalance to a supply imbalance. No rules or numbers.

## 5. Shorting in an uptrend (pp.174-177)

- A rally that is **nearing big supply** is where "almost all uptrends end" and new downtrends begin. Short at supply even in an uptrend: "there is a time and a place to sell short during an uptrend".
- **Novice-emotion candle sign:** in a series of rising green candles, the **largest candles come at the end of the series**. That is emotional late buying into supply (candle "A" on the NASDAQ intraday chart).
- Supply pattern = **Rally-Base-Drop**. Draw two lines around the base, the "supply zone", and wait for the return.
- The HTF shows the turning point; the LTF shows the novices piling in.

## 6. Worked ES short: plan the whole trade, then leave (pp.178-182)

- Your job is to *search* for opportunity, not to trade every day. Execute "like a robot", enter the whole trade (entry, stop, target) and walk away. Watching the screen keeps "the largest risk", your emotion, alive.
- **A = supply**, pattern **RBD**. **A Drop-Base-Drop is NOT treated as supply**: "found in the middle of moves and typically don't work well".
- **B = rate of decline** from A: a faster decline means a bigger imbalance and **higher probability**.
- **C = the rally back** into A. "Strong rally with no sign of demand within that rally" is **the most inviting** part. A steep rally in tends to fall back through the level at nearly the same rate.
- **D = entry**: short at **911** as price reached supply after the open.
- **E = stop**: **915**, just above the supply zone. **4 ES points = $200/contract; 5 contracts = $1,000 risk.**
- **F = target**: **899 = 12 points = 3:1**. Exit some or all there; 5 contracts = **$3,000**. "Keep goals ≥ 3:1"; he says this also raises probability.

## 7. Pay attention: ES supply zone (pp.182-185)

- Base = "price was not moving much" (balance), then **one big red candle** collapsed price. Draw two lines around the cluster at the origin. That is the "supply/sell zone", planned hours before the open.
- Student example: entered at 923, stopped at 923.75 (−0.75) after manually tightening the stop, re-entered, and exited at **909.75 (+13.25)**. Lesson: assess risk first, then reward.

## 8. S/D for your nest egg: currency risk and hedging (pp.186-190)

- You can chart and trade S/D on anything, mutual funds included. Schwab Tech Fund: buy at "pivot low support" demand.
- **Currency risk:** a USD asset can rise while its buying power falls. Watch the **US Dollar Index** at supply. If the DXY reaches supply, USD assets are about to lose buying power.
- **Hedge:** if the SPY reaches **first-revisit supply**, short the SPY, short ES or buy SPX puts to protect a stock portfolio.

## 9. Lemonade stand / AA short (pp.190-193)

- AA short at **12.08** at daily supply (RBD, "a pivot high"). **T1 = 10.85 (3:1), T2 = 10.03 (5:1).** This implies a stop of about 12.49.
- Car analogy: buying after a rally is like paying $40k for a $30k car, and buying into supply is like stepping on the gas at a concrete wall.

## 10. Breakout entries (pp.193-198)

- Buying the breakout itself is the "sucker bet": by the time it breaks, risk is high and reward low. Let the breakout **prove the imbalance**, then buy the **return to the origin**.
- **Setup (long):**
  - **Trend filter: a 20-period moving average** shows an uptrend.
  - The origin of the strong move must be **Drop-Base-Rally**. It must be preceded by a decline, which gives higher probability than RBR.
  - Draw two lines (zone A).
  - The profit margin is the distance from A to **B, the highest high of the initial breakout** before price returns. It must be "significant".
- **Action:** buy at C when price touches the **top line (proximal)**. Put the **stop just below the lower line (distal)**. Size the position by your money risk. Put the **target at the breakout high B** (exit at D).
- The mirror image applies to breakdowns. It works on any market and any TF. You must also know where price sits on the **larger-TF S/D curve**.

## 11. FAQ: GENZ critique (pp.198-201)

- It takes longest to learn when you have lots of *bad* prior experience. Use a "simple logic filter".
- Why the GENZ long was low odds:
  1. **Supply not far above** limited the profit and meant sellers were close. There was also a "pocket of sellers" the student missed.
  2. It was the **third time** price returned to the level, and the latest rally away was weak.
  3. **The S&P was not at demand.** "Timing your trade to the S&P is not everything but it sure is important."
- Books: Economics 101 (S/D) and Psychology 101 (how people decide).

## 12. Same tools, different thoughts (pp.201-205)

- **Trend health:** in an uptrend, measure the distance between successive **pivot lows** (pullbacks). If the **spacing is shrinking** (pullbacks come more often and closer together), the trend is weakening and likely to end soon, because a strong trend does not pull back often. This works on any TF.
- **Moving-average cross as a level finder:** do not trade the cross, because it lags. A cross happens *because* of a strong move, so **look slightly to the left of a past MA cross** to find the origin of that move, likely a demand or supply level. (He says he does not use these two himself; the point is to think differently.)

## 13. Where most people get it wrong (pp.205-210)

- Conventional TA and fundamental analysis make you buy after the rally or after the good news, which has no edge.
- The pizza-cheese logic: buy as much as you can when the price is far below average, and only what you need when it is high.
- Aug 19: the NASDAQ gapped down into demand at the open, and CSCO had demand lined up with the NASDAQ demand. Long both at "wholesale" prices. Analysis started **1 hour before the US open**.

## 14. Foolsville, Part 1 (pp.210-217)

- Sept 1: SPY supply above (the level was created by a **gap down**). The financial sector had the **same supply level**, with GS and C as the best candidates. The open was below the level, so they needed a rally *into* it. Short there.
- This is an example of index, sector and stock all lining up at supply.

## 15. Motion into mass (pp.217-222)

- Mass = resting orders, from his CME floor experience: the largest stack of sell orders above price stops a rally.
- **The first revisit** of a supply that price gapped away from → very high odds; short it (A).
- **The second revisit (B)** has less supply, because each visit removes or absorbs orders. It was still shorted, because price **gapped into** the level (novice buying).
- **Revisits C and D:** the declines got **more and more shallow**, a sign the supply was absorbed. **No shorts there**: shorting was now high risk because price was likely to break through.
- **E:** price traded deep into the level ("hardly a level anymore"). **Switch sides:** look for **demand on a smaller TF (the 5-minute)** below price and buy. Risk is low, and reward is high because the overhead supply has been absorbed.

## 16. Losing to win (pp.223-228)

- A loss belongs in the "winning" category, because it is part of the process.
- **Size so the loss is a "tiny percentage" of the account.** In one lower-risk account he keeps losses at **$1,000 or less**; the AAPL loss was **$960**. If the money loss makes you cringe, the size is too big.
- AAPL: supply A was shorted, with a small profit at first, then it was stopped out. Price ran to the next supply C, and he **shorted again**.
- **At 3:1 the stop was moved to breakeven** ("a free trade").
- Day result: 2 ES trades, 1 win and 1 loss (**50% accuracy**), net **+$1,562.50**. "Like Las Vegas."
- "To become a professional winner, you have to first become a professional loser."

## 17. Q&A (pp.228-233)

- **FESX (Euro Stoxx 50, Eurex):** there is no overnight session, so it **gaps almost daily** ("we love gaps"). **1 point = €10.**
- **Scaling in vs adding to a loser:** scaling is fine **only if it is pre-planned**. Know every entry and the single stop, then size the total risk. **Add larger size the closer you get to the stop.** Unplanned adding = adding to a loser.
- **Position trading** uses the same method on larger TFs. The key is **large profit margins**.
- **VIX as an odds enhancer:**
  - VIX high and at **supply** + stock market at **demand** → expect a rally.
  - VIX low and at **demand** + market at **supply** → expect a decline.
  - Check it **once a day for day traders, once a week for swing/position traders**. A delayed chart is fine.
- **Fresh vs historical S/R:** do not count touches. Look for **fresh** levels (*not revisited since they formed*), even if you must look **far back**.
  - **Older fresh levels are better**: they sit far out on the curve ("the rubber band is very stretched").
  - He will accept **a little more risk** on them because of the probability and margin.

## 18. Foolsville, Part 2 (pp.233-240)

- This is a parody. The "Fool" system scores **7 criteria × 10 = 70**: green candles, uptrend, good news, good earnings and so on. Its best scores happen when price is very high. The point: who buys after you? This is the anti-pattern of conventional confirmation.
- Sept 11: pre-market news (a China-US trade dispute) caused selling. QQQQ had demand below the prior day's close. They bought at demand while the "Fool" shouted "sell short". Price moved significantly higher.
- Foolsville belief: "Make decisions based upon the actions of others, after they make it."

### Candidate rules for the repo (suggestions only; I did not change any code)
1. **Sector or index alignment:** add an optional check that the correlated index (FX: DXY) sits at a matching HTF level.
2. **Pivot-spacing trend health:** if the bar distance between the last 3 pullback pivots shrinks, mark the trend as "weakening" and lower the with-trend score.
3. **Shallower-reaction detector:** after a test, measure each reaction leg. If each one is smaller than the last, cancel fades of that zone and look for LTF zones in the break direction.
4. **Seiden breakeven preset:** move the stop to BE at 3R instead of at 0.5-1R.
5. **Planned scale-in:** split entries inside the zone, with more size near the distal line and one shared stop.
6. **Breakout-return template:** a 20-MA trend filter, a DBR/RBD origin only, and the target at the high/low of the initial breakout.
