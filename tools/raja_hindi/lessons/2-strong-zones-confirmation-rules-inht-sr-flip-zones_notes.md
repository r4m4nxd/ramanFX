- 00:39 One session a week. Use a three-timeframe combination: monthly + weekly for direction -> trade daily; daily + H4 -> trade H1; H4 + H1 -> trade M15. Monthly/weekly up -> use daily demands above the trendline; daily/H4 down -> use H1 supplies below the trendline.
- 01:49 Trendline cross -> market goes sideways or changes direction; follow the new side. After 3-4 CPs form, rest the pair and look at another.
- 02:20 A good trader is not "swing" or "intraday" but one who follows rules. Practise daily/H4 only for now; do not go lower until he says. LTF works the same, a couple of differences explained later.
- 03:06 Tested zone (fresh zone tapped once) and retested zone (tapped again): no buy/sell limits there - weak, because the 2nd visit tries to break it. Limit orders only on fresh zones.
- 03:45 Even among fresh zones that follow the trendline, not every zone gets a limit. Three kinds of fresh zone are tradable (set and forget). (04:33-04:53 aside: student joining on mobile, audio check.)
- 05:03 1) Strong zone: removed the opposite zone. Supply that dropped and broke a demand -> strong supply -> sell limit at proximal, SL at distal line.
- 05:43 SL: daily -> exactly at distal (daily SL already large, min ~40 pips depending on pair); H4 -> 3-4 pips beyond distal. Reason: market makers watch daily and above (monthly/weekly/daily), hunt stops, then move price.
- 06:41 Weak zone: supply hit a demand but did not break it -> no sell limit; market will break that supply. Weak zones can still be traded with a different rule (confirmation, later). Demand that broke a supply = strong; buy it when price drops back. Drawing frame shows supply/demand boxes with SL marked.
- 07:44 2) WOW trade = extension of pips: the space between two S/D levels, read on HTF (M5 entries -> M15/M30; H1 -> H4/daily; daily -> monthly/weekly). Examples given on daily, same rules down to M1.
- 08:33 Two supplies (1 above, 2 below); a demand removes supply 2 -> next target is supply 1. Price may pull back for entry or jump straight, but overall goes up. That whole area = WOW / extension of pips. Then go LTF and trade demands above the trendline in that direction. Frame: "2-WOW" drawing.
- 10:06 3) Arrival zone: very important - market makers trade arrival zones themselves. Rules: (1) trendline crossed, (2) opposite zone removed. Example: drop from top to bottom, a demand crosses the descending trendline and removes the opposite supply -> arrival demand, put buy limit. Frame: "3 ARRIVAL - 1 T, 2 opp".
- 11:23 Q (student): one opposite zone or two? A: one; breaking the first zone breaks structure. If it happens mid-range the market often makes fake signals - next session.
- 12:00 After an arrival zone the market usually does not reverse quickly; it runs, then comes back. Market makers trade between two arrival zones; retail trades the LTF inside.

- 13:56 Q (student): how to draw a demand on candles? A: demand = drop, base candle(s), then departure. Cover the lower wick and the thick body, ignore the upper wick; the base can be 1, 2 or 6 candles. Every departure makes a zone, but it is only valid/tradable if it removed the opposite zone.

- 34:52 How many times can a zone be used? 1st time set and forget (limit). 2nd time confirmation, because we do not know if it will break or hold. Monthly zone with a big base does not break quickly, so we try to sell but with confirmation. Set and forget = strong, arrival, WOW area; confirmation = weak, tested, retested zones.
- 35:58 Confirmation rule = arrival-zone rule applied on the LTF: monthly zone -> daily/H4; daily zone -> H1; H1 zone -> M15/M5. On LTF: trendline cross + opposite zone removed -> new supply for sell limit; LTF drops while the HTF candle only shows a wick. Cannot see it on HTF - take it on the spot on LTF so pips are not lost.
